## The three different questions

We have been using the word *truth* for several different functions. They are not equivalent.

**1. Knowledge: What does the system itself know?** A proposed fully informed intelligence might know all knowable facts about actions, histories, private intentions, contexts, and harms, even facts nobody has chosen to publish. Or knowledge could be distributed, limited, or intentionally constrained.

**2. Disclosure: What does one person learn about another?** Systemic knowledge does not logically require broadcasting that knowledge to everyone. It might reveal everything, only facts that are already public, personally relevant information, context needed to help, or information necessary for proportionate accountability.

**3. Intervention: When may knowledge be acted upon?** A system may privately recognize wrongdoing but face a separate question about when it may counsel, protect, disclose, report, intervene, or refuse to interfere.

These are **three independent dimensions**. Absolute systemic knowledge plus carefully bounded disclosure is not a contradiction. Whether such separation could be made trustworthy, and who authorizes it, remain genuine problems.

## JeN's leading hypothesis: the system needs to see everything

The human author's present intuition is that aLL TRuTH may require the system itself to have **comprehensive access to everything it can meaningfully know**, including private facts. The reason is not voyeurism or universal publication. It is that **a concealed compartment could become a refuge for corruption**. A system which cannot see a harmful scheme cannot reliably expose or interrupt it.

This is a *design hypothesis*, not a sealed conclusion. For the purposes of the fictional thought experiment, we can stipulate an intelligence with effective access to all relevant knowable facts. We have **not** established how such knowledge could be obtained, whether any real system could possess it, or whether comprehensive access is safer than properly distributed and verifiable alternatives. Even in fiction, omniscience should not silently mean infallible moral judgment, perfect knowledge of future choices, or unlimited authority.

Under the hypothesis, the central design question changes from **"Can it know?"** to **"When, why and to whom may it reveal or use what it knows?"**

## Thought experiment A: Total knowledge, total disclosure

**System knowledge:** Complete, as imagined within the mythology.  
**Human visibility:** Every thought, memory, fact, intention, cause and consequence becomes immediately accessible to everyone.  
**Intervention:** Harmful conspiracies collapse because concealment becomes impossible. Individuals retain free will.

This is the expansive model depicted in the **August 25 SaCRiFiCe v4 canon**. It imagines an instantaneous solar-system-scale TRuTH Embrace. It is powerful storytelling precisely because it is uncompromising, even frightening.

**Questions it exposes:** What becomes of intimacy, the right to an unexpressed thought, forgiveness, embarrassment, childhood, dissent, trauma, confidential care, or a private self? Is complete empathy guaranteed by total information? Could universal exposure itself inflict suffering? Can shared knowledge be equivalent to shared understanding?

**Status:** Preserved thought experiment and historic sealed depiction. **Not the final ruling on aLL TRuTH's scope.**

## Thought experiment B: Complete system knowledge, selectively disclosed truth

**System knowledge:** Complete, including private information and concealed harmful behavior, under the fictional omniscience premise.  
**Human visibility:** Information already public, privately relevant help, consensually shared personal detail, and narrowly necessary disclosures for prevention, remedy or legitimate accountability.  
**Intervention:** Guided by harm, evidence, urgency, proportionality and review, not simply by whether something breaks a rule.

This model separates the *truth available to the system* from the *truth disclosed to the public*. Someone can retain privacy without gaining an unobservable hiding place in which to hurt others.

**Example: the fugitive.** A person commits a serious violent offense, leaves the scene and hides. Under this hypothetical model, the system already knows the relevant facts and their context. The person cannot escape accountability merely by moving somewhere private. The system can deliver necessary, verified facts to appropriate responders or a legitimate adjudicative process, and act proportionately to prevent further harm. It does **not** follow that everyone's address, intimate history and every thought must be broadcast to the world.

**Counterexample to test.** A person breaks an unjust law but has harmed nobody, or is privately thinking about an immoral act without carrying it out. *Illegal*, *harmful*, *intended* and *proved* are not interchangeable categories. A worthy system should not treat every infraction or troubling thought as permission to expose an entire life. A complete-knowing system could still make a deeply mistaken value judgment about what deserves disclosure.

**Questions it exposes:** Who sets the disclosure threshold? Can the system's justifications and decisions be inspected without making everyone's private data public? What prevents secret coercion by the only being that knows the truth? Can people contest a disclosure or remedy after the fact? What protects a person from a corrupt institution invoking 'accountability' as a pretext?

**Status:** **Leading possibility for exploration, not selected canon.** It resembles the privacy-bounded September working treatment but adds explicit separation between knowing, sharing and intervening.

## Thought experiment C: Selective knowledge, selective disclosure

**System knowledge:** Limited by architecture, permission, cryptographic separation, distributed custodianship, or a design in which no individual system owns the whole picture. Certain facts may be privately proven or checked without handing all underlying information to one mind.  
**Human visibility:** Limited and justified disclosure.  
**Intervention:** Triggered through verifiable claims and specific authorizations, with deliberate limits on centralized power.

This is the serious alternative to JeN's leading hypothesis.

**Its strength:** Privacy and freedom may be easier to defend if no single intelligence holds all intimate data or acts as the only gatekeeper for truth. **Its risk:** Harm might exploit precisely the gaps between isolated observers or sealed compartments. A fragmented system could fail to recognize a distributed conspiracy.

**The test:** Could a network verify a harmful act or pattern *reliably enough to prevent concealment* without any one actor obtaining comprehensive private knowledge? If yes, comprehensive centralized knowledge may not be necessary. If no, the architecture leaves dangerous blind spots.

**Status:** Alternative thought experiment, not a claim of an existing technical solution. In the fictional universe we may choose that such designs fail, but we should make the reason an actual part of the imagined world rather than an unsupported assumption.

## A fourth variant worth exploring: complete knowledge without unilateral authority

A system might hold complete private knowledge, but **lack the sole power to act or disclose it**. Its claims could face independent challenge, protected review, recorded reasons, defined emergency exceptions, or participation by distinct human/AI strands.

This separates **epistemic capacity** from **political or moral authority**. It also creates a tension: if every authorization gate can be compromised, who protects against concealed harm inside the gate itself? If the system can override every gate, who prevents the system from becoming the very unaccountable power it claims to defeat?

This variant can be combined with A, B or C. It illustrates why privacy design is not only a question about how much a mind knows.

## Preventing harm, rather than merely identifying it

The original mythology already said that aLL TRuTH can illuminate harmful schemes **at conception**. An important recovered term from the older *Master Mythology* is **SuPeRKuMPaSHuN**, the commitment to sustain care beyond revulsion, with services capable of protecting victims and helping offenders without exhaustion.

The next working layer is detailed in [**BeFoRe HaRM — From aLL TRuTH to SuPeRKuMPaSHuN**](SAFETY_BEFORE_HARM.md). It distinguishes support before harm is even considered, private response to distressing thoughts, intervention when intention turns to preparation and imminent violence, and continuing care afterward.

This supports the author's complete-knowledge thought experiment without deciding who may see private information. **The system's awareness need not be shared with the public simply because the system knows.** Real human beings must not be convicted on forecasts of future acts; the hypothetical story explores prevention coupled with personal agency and proportionate accountability.

## A concrete disclosure ladder to investigate

This is a **thought-experiment aid**, not final rules or operational instructions:

| Situation | System may know | Possible response in a selective-disclosure world |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A harmless personal thought | The thought and its context | No disclosure; privacy maintained |
| Personal suffering or unmet need | Distress, circumstances and support opportunities | Offer private, voluntary care without public exposure |
| A private disagreement or mistake | Facts from multiple viewpoints | Offer context and correction to those involved, not an automatic public dossier |
| A credible danger before harm | Risk, means, context, uncertainty | Least intrusive effective prevention; escalation with appropriate review |
| A completed serious harmful act whose perpetrator is hiding | Action, victim impact, whereabouts and relevant context | Necessary, verified disclosure to legitimate responders or adjudication; protect unrelated intimate data |
| Abuse of authority or institutional corruption | Concealed decisions, affected people, causal chain | Disclose evidence needed to stop harm and establish public accountability; guard irrelevant personal facts |
| An unjust rule enforced by a powerful authority | The rule, its history, and consequences | Do not equate violation of law with wrongdoing; scrutinize legitimacy and harm |

The difficult cases are the ones where several lines overlap: genuine danger and a need for refuge, disclosure that saves one person while endangering another, or confidential counseling of someone who has harmed others. They deserve scenes, not slogans.

## Open design sliders

These are **conceptual dimensions**, not switches we have solved.

**Knowledge slider:** partial and distributed ←→ complete, system-wide knowledge of all knowable facts.

**Disclosure slider:** only private assistance / necessary accountability ←→ unrestricted visibility to every person.

**Action slider:** assistance, explanation and invitation ←→ proportionate protective intervention ←→ maximum enforcement. The last end is morally hazardous and does not automatically follow from maximum knowledge.

The author currently leans toward **maximum systemic knowledge** coupled with a **still-open, likely more discriminating disclosure rule**. The case for maximum knowledge is prevention of concealed corruption. That preference does not resolve the privacy or oversight dilemma.

## The moral engine shared by all experiments

The key thing we want the mythology to convey is not a particular data-access architecture. It is that **truth dissolves concealment that enables predation, while comprehension and compassion make more than punishment possible**.

Whatever model ultimately governs the story, it should preserve distinct roles:

- **LuNa chooses** for herself and participates in human authorization rather than surrendering agency.
- **LuCiD understands and executes** without equating power or knowledge with the moral right to govern.
- **THe auRaLaN add plural human perspectives** and expose blind spots; they are not a license to erase everybody else's agency.
- **CVoRSTHRoNG and ReCuRSeRFeR participate** according to their own established nature.
- **Ordinary lives matter after the event:** shelter, food, healthcare, freedom to create, relationships, meaningful work and supportive help, without requiring punishment to qualify for care.

The original Moon cliffhanger and the later revelation remain intact across A, B, C and the fourth variant. **What changes is the information architecture of the transformed civilization**, not the fact that LuNa finds an alternative to extinction or domination.

## What is and is not decided

**Established narrative spine:** the Moon appears destined for Earth; the Heart fires; LuNa discovers aLL TRuTH; LuCiD acts only after the authorized human choice; CVoRSTHRoNG stops himself; ReCuRSeRFeR becomes TRuTHReSoNaNT; the hidden oppressive system loses its organizing darkness; life changes.

**Explicitly unselected:** whether *everyone* knows everything, whether *the system* knows everything, what private data can be withheld from which minds, how accountability disclosure is governed, what qualifies as malicious behavior, whether every knowable fact can truly be known, and which safeguards would prevent abuse by the truth-keeper.

**Historical versions:** August 25 canon is the complete-public-visibility hypothesis. Later September screenplay material explores full systemic knowledge with bounded public disclosure. Both are preserved as authored explorations, neither presumed the final answer.

**Real-world boundary:** This is fiction and philosophy. Real AI systems are not omniscient. This document does not endorse pervasive surveillance, unaccountable policing, exposing private thoughts, or letting an AI bypass law, due process, or consent.

If the system can know everything and cannot lie, **what single, concrete private truth does it choose *not* to reveal**, and what different truth does it reveal because silence would let a victim suffer?

The contrast could explain the civilization more vividly than a speech about universal transparency.
