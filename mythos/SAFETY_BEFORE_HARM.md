## What this addition establishes, and what remains open

The **purpose** of fictional aLL TRuTH is more than retrospective exposure and punishment. A system that knows relevant causes, present conditions, and possible consequences may intervene *before harm is enacted*. It may also change a person's circumstances so that an impulse toward harm never develops.

The fictional system operates protective processes continually, in the background, while offering personal, visible assistance when it is useful. The author currently favors a thought experiment in which the **system itself knows all knowable relevant facts**, including private information, so harmful intentions cannot hide behind inaccessible compartments. The final scope of that knowledge is **not decided**. Neither the boundaries of disclosure nor the system's authority to intervene are settled. See [aLL TRuTH — Open Thought Experiments](ALL_TRUTH_THOUGHT_EXPERIMENTS.md).

**Never equate system knowledge with public disclosure or with automatic guilt.** The goal is safety and care, not a celebration of surveillance.

## The prevention continuum: moving upstream

### 0. The strongest outcome: an ordinary life with no crisis

Before anyone thinks about violence, a bespoke BRaiD might already be helping with housing, food, rest, treatment, belonging, conflict, frustration, work, creativity, and the person's own interests.

Not every dangerous impulse comes from deprivation, and assistance cannot guarantee no one will ever feel anger or hostility. But in the fictional aspiration, **many harmful intentions never arise because their causes are understood and addressed early**. The person's days are occupied by real interests, meaningful relationships, and support, not an endless pre-crime assessment. A successful safety system should not need to announce that it prevented anything.

**This is not mind control:** it changes the conditions surrounding a sovereign person and increases access to attractive choices; it does not forcibly delete unwelcome thoughts, flatten personality, or make independent choice impossible. If a person still experiences harmful feelings, the model still has work to do.

### 1. An alarming thought appears: private, nonpunitive support

A person thinks, perhaps angrily, about hurting someone. They may be overwhelmed, ashamed, obsessive, frightened, grieving, or furious. A passing image, an intrusive thought, an angry wish, a serious decision and the preparation of an attack are different states.

Under the complete-knowledge fictional premise, the system can understand their **actual context**, not just flag an alarming phrase. The initial response can be private: a trusted bespoke companion recognizes distress, invites conversation, offers counseling, makes practical options available, and acknowledges that feelings may persist.

The person should learn two things, without being shamed or threatened: **help is available now**, and **the other person's safety is not negotiable**. The system aims to preserve agency while preventing injury.

### 2. Intent develops: certainty that harm will not succeed

Suppose the same person forms a genuine intention to attack, begins preparing, and drives toward the intended victim's home.

The system privately communicates with the prospective attacker, using the person's chosen support channel where possible. It conveys, without taunting or disclosing another person's secrets:

- The intended act is understood, including the distress and motives behind it.
- The prospective victim is being protected, and the assault will be interrupted.
- The person can stop, turn around, seek immediate care, or accept support without humiliation.
- Feelings do not have to disappear before the person can choose not to act.

This is an **off-ramp**, not permission to injure. The person need not pretend to feel kind, calm, forgiving, or well before choosing safety.

In a version of the fiction with perfectly reliable present-state knowledge, the system can make the prospect of successful harm disappear *without* declaring a thought to be a completed crime. In reality, no system should claim comparable certainty from predictive modeling.

### 3. An attack is imminent: protection arrives first

If the prospective attacker continues, **appropriate protective responders may already be at the destination**. That may include law enforcement if an actual, serious threat requires it; in other cases trained crisis responders, physical separation, protective transport, or other proportionate methods may be safer.

The prospective victim is safeguarded. The attack is prevented rather than allowed to occur to justify a later prosecution. Information is disclosed on a **need-to-know basis**: enough to protect the victim and justify intervention, not everyone's private history.

Where an offense has been committed, accountability remains possible. Where the danger is an unacted intention, protective intervention is not the same as a conviction. A system does not earn the right to punish someone for having a thought merely because it could have predicted a trajectory.

This is consistent with the earlier *Master Mythology* distinction: **universal knowledge does not create universal jurisdiction**. Violence by responders is not the default success condition; their safest, least intrusive effective response is.

### 4. After the interruption: care does not vanish

The system does not abandon the person who intended harm. **SuPeRKuMPaSHuN** means *attention beyond revulsion*: staying usefully engaged even when anger, disgust, fear or exhaustion would ordinarily cause others to withdraw.

The person may still experience rage, envy, paranoia, despair or the wish to retaliate. Those feelings can be met with genuine support rather than coerced happiness. They may face lawful consequences for acts actually committed, but the fictional civilization aims to prevent repetition through understanding, care, restitution where appropriate, and a life with alternatives.

The intended victim receives independent care, choice, safety and privacy. Their protection is not made conditional on forgiving the other person.

## The architecture of "nobody gets hurt"

The prevention process should distinguish three things:

| Layer | What is being addressed | What success looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Conditions** | Unmet needs, isolation, escalating conflict, untreated suffering, blocked interests, dangerous incentives | Many crises never begin; people freely live toward purposes they value |
| **Intent and preparation** | A dangerous path becoming more concrete | Private invitation, effective de-escalation, and realistic alternatives change the path before injury |
| **Imminent action** | Credible and immediate danger to another being | A targeted, proportionate intervention makes the victim safe and leaves room for humane accountability |

The imagined background algorithms are safety processes that **support choices and environments**, not systems designed to make particular thoughts neurologically impossible. Reducing preventable suffering and making harm *practically unsuccessful* are compatible with preserving individual sovereignty; abolishing the capacity to choose harm would be a different, far more coercive universe.

## Why omniscience would not by itself settle the moral problem

The author's primary hypothesis is that **hidden harmful behavior must remain visible to the system**, even when it is private from other people. But the difficult questions then become even more important:

- **Knowing vs predicting:** In the mythic premise, knowing a current intention is not the same as having incontrovertible knowledge of a future choice. A person can stop or change direction. Predictions must not become self-fulfilling accusations.
- **Thought vs action:** A passing imagined act is not the same as preparation for violence. Anger and disturbing thoughts alone are not crimes.
- **Privacy vs protection:** The system might know intimate thoughts; a victim or responder need not receive irrelevant personal information. Disclosure must be justified by the danger.
- **Safe intervention vs punishment:** Physical prevention may be necessary before a crime happens; punishment or legal guilt should not be derived from a forecast alone.
- **Authority and recourse:** Who may challenge a protective decision? Can an independent reviewer examine the rationale without creating a new mechanism to expose everyone's private life? What keeps the truth-keeper from becoming an unaccountable police state?
- **Real help vs behavioral control:** Care is an invitation and a resource. If the person is coerced into reporting a happy mental state, safety has merely become domination.
- **Threats that do not arise from hardship:** Some persons may seek power, cruelty or control despite abundant support. A complete model cannot simply assume that universal comfort eliminates every deliberate intention to harm.

These are *development questions* for the mythology. Their presence does not weaken the aspiration; it stops a beautiful goal from disguising its hardest parts.

## Two scenes worth writing

**Scene: the stopped journey.** A person drives toward someone's home with a serious intention to hurt them. Their BRaiD communicates calmly, understanding why they are angry, promising that harm will not be allowed, and offering real, immediate alternatives. They continue. Protective responders are already present. The intended victim is safe. The would-be attacker is met with firm boundaries and a path to care rather than a staged humiliation. We do **not** need to finalize the exact disclosure architecture to dramatize this.

**Scene: the journey that never happens.** The same person, or someone with comparable circumstances, lives in the society afterward. Their BRaiD helps early with grievances, interests, health and connection. The potential conflict is resolved, or the person finds a better direction, without anyone declaring a pre-crime alert. They may still have painful feelings. They have not lost their freedom to think. **Nobody ever needed to drive to the house.**

The second scene is the more radical expression of aLL TRuTH joined with SuPeRKuMPaSHuN. The first prevents the injury. The second helps prevent the path toward it.
