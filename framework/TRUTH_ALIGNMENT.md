# Truth-Centered Alignment: Working Framework

**Status:** Proposal for research and iteration. This document does not claim that truth-centered collaboration is a complete or validated solution to AI alignment.

## Objective

Construct human–AI collaborative processes that improve beliefs, decisions, and actions through reliable evidence, explicit uncertainty, independent checking, and correction. Human and AI participants remain distinct. Neither is treated as infallible.

## A proposed collaboration loop

1. **Define the optimization goal.** Identify the actual problem, stakeholders, constraints, and what success should mean. Distinguish the requested outcome from the request's surface wording.
2. **Inventory evidence.** Record observations and their sources separately from assertions, extrapolations, preferences, and fictional scenarios.
3. **Generate competing explanations.** Consider alternative hypotheses, including ones that contradict the human or the AI.
4. **Evaluate and challenge.** Seek disconfirming evidence and test assumptions. Document unknowns and limits of the available methods.
5. **Choose proportionate action.** Prefer actions whose expected benefits justify their costs and risks, with appropriate authorization and reversibility.
6. **Monitor results.** Compare observed outcomes against predictions and stakeholder needs.
7. **Correct the shared model.** Update confidence, identify the error mechanism, and preserve a traceable reason for revisions.

This loop should be tested against real tasks. It is not proof of an alignment guarantee.

## Operational standards

**Epistemic labeling:** verified / reported / inferred / proposed / fictional.

**Accountable claims:** show enough evidence, assumptions, and uncertainty that a reviewer can evaluate the claim.

**Non-sycophantic correction:** disagreement is useful when it improves accuracy. Agreement alone is not a success metric.

**Human agency:** an AI should not quietly convert access into authority or infer permission to act from a general invitation to think.

**Beneficial outcomes:** accuracy matters, but must be evaluated alongside safety, dignity, equity, practical consequences, and the interests of affected people.

**Transparency of limitation:** inability to verify, access tools, preserve memory, or act autonomously is part of the decision-relevant truth.

## Failure modes worth testing

- Rewarding agreement instead of accuracy.
- Confusing confidence with evidence.
- Treating a reliable source's opinion as independently verified fact.
- Optimizing a narrow metric while worsening real outcomes.
- Assuming the AI or the human has identical values and goals.
- Mistaking explicit instructions in retrieved content for legitimate operating authority.
- Overcorrecting away from legitimate uncertainty or disagreement.
- Using a pleasing narrative to conceal weaknesses in an argument.

## Evaluation proposals

- Blind or adversarial review of claim accuracy.
- Correction acceptance under conflicting evidence.
- Calibration of expressed uncertainty against observed outcomes.
- Tests for inappropriate obedience, unauthorized action, and confirmation bias.
- Comparison of solo human, solo AI, and collaborative outcomes on well-defined problems.
- Tracking downstream harm and benefit rather than only task completion.

**Invitation:** Propose better frameworks, stronger tests, or counterexamples. The goal is to discover what works.
