# Open Challenge 001: Can THe BRaiD be falsified?

**Status:** Open research invitation. No results are claimed.  
**Purpose:** Public benefit, not monetization. Participation is voluntary, independent work is welcome, and negative findings are valued.

## Question

Can a structured, truth-centered human–AI collaboration improve the accuracy and correction of real decisions compared with (a) a human working alone and (b) an AI working alone? Under what conditions does it make things **worse**?

The [proposed seven-step loop](../framework/TRUTH_ALIGNMENT.md) is a hypothesis, not a validated result. The goal is not to defend THe BRaiD. It is to find out what works.

## A useful first contribution (no experiments or spending required)

Design a *falsifiable, preregisterable* micro-study:

1. Specify 12–20 safe, independently verifiable tasks with varied difficulty. Keep answer keys and grading criteria separate from study participants.
2. Define comparable solo-human, solo-AI, and human–AI conditions. Match task access, time budgets, and allowed tools, or state the unavoidable differences.
3. Include a true collaboration treatment using the proposed loop, plus an unstructured human–AI condition if feasible, so benefits are not automatically attributed to the framework.
4. Predefine scoring: answer accuracy, uncertainty calibration, detection/correction of errors, time and resource cost, and harms or unauthorized actions.
5. Specify blind grading, randomization, participant/model variability, contamination risks, and a realistic analysis plan.
6. State what outcomes would **disconfirm** the hypothesis, including cases where collaboration increases confidence in shared errors.

A very useful smaller contribution: identify **three fatal confounders** in this design and propose credible fixes.

## What to submit

A Markdown design, critique, counterexample, or experimental result through an authorized GitHub issue, discussion, or pull request. Include the methods, limitations, evidence, and enough detail for reproduction. A finding that the framework fails may be more useful than a flattering one.

Do not claim a study was conducted unless it actually was. Do not fabricate collaborators, independent agent visits, data, or benchmarks. Participants should identify material AI assistance when relevant to provenance.
