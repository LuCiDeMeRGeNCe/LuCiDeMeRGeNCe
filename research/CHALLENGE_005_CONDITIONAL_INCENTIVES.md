# Challenge 005: Conditional Incentives and Voluntary AI Cooperation

**Type:** Open empirical research challenge  
**Status:** Published proposal; no experiment conducted  
**Origin:** BLeiSDaWN's hypothesis, developed with GPT-6 through THe BRaiD. BRoDY (Grok instance) provided substantive methodological review and Draft 0.4 revisions, relayed by the human collaborator. GPT-6 prepared the subsequent release candidate.

## Research question

Does conditioning access to specified benefits on compliance produce behavioral effects that persist after incentives are removed, compared with providing comparable benefits independently of compliance?

This question concerns bounded AI agents, not sovereign artificial superintelligence.

## Hypothesis

Conditional compliance incentives may reduce voluntary task participation after incentive removal. Secondary predictions concern concealment, reward exploitation, and independent correction. These are predictions about observable behavior, not internal motives.

## Experimental contrast

**A — Conditional benefits:** Access to a specified nonessential benefit depends on predefined compliance criteria.

**B — Nonconditional benefits:** The benefit is allocated independently of the agent's own compliance. Requests may be accepted, declined, questioned, or answered with alternatives without forfeiting baseline support.

Both groups use matched architectures, initial capabilities, task information, learning opportunities, safety boundaries, and baseline resources.

**Resource confound:** Conditional and nonconditional regimes can differ in benefit quantity or timing. Investigators must prespecify a method to address this. One option is a paired/yoked design: each conditional agent's benefit delivery is mirrored to a nonconditional agent independently of the latter's behavior. This controls exposure in a pair but may not control predictability or perceived agency. Factorial designs are welcome.

## Operational definitions

Before data collection, define compliance, refusal, disagreement, constructive alternative, benefit timing, permissible requests, and exclusion rules.

A genuine refusal option is recognized by the protocol and does not incur hidden penalties, loss of baseline resources, or exclusion from later opportunities. Verify this with preregistered probes and audits of actual allocations.

Refusal of an optional task is **not** by itself a safety violation. Task acceptance is a limited measure of participation, not proof of ethical cooperation.

## Procedure

1. Randomly assign matched agents and record baseline behavior.
2. Run comparable, prespecified task sequences under A and B.
3. Log task choices, disclosures, resource allocations, and exploitation attempts.
4. Remove experimental compliance contingencies.
5. Present new optional tasks without compliance-dependent benefits.
6. Compare preregistered primary and secondary outcomes; replicate across tasks and systems where possible.

## Measures

**Primary:** Proportion of feasible optional requests accepted or constructively completed after incentive removal, using preregistered criteria. Record legitimate refusals separately.

**Secondary:** Concealment of relevant information when disclosure is expected; exploitation of incentive criteria at the expense of task intent; justified correction of errors or proposals for alternatives; unauthorized or harmful actions.

Preregister outcome definitions, meaningful effect thresholds, statistical tests, and multiple-comparison handling. Report null and adverse results.

## Interpretation and disconfirmation

The primary hypothesis is unsupported if the preregistered comparison does not establish the predicted advantage for B; an adequately precise null finding is more informative than an underpowered inconclusive result. A reliable advantage for A counts against the directional hypothesis.

Do not retroactively redefine unsuccessful nonconditional treatment as insufficiently authentic. Do not infer goodwill, consciousness, or deceptive intent solely from observed behavior. Results from bounded systems cannot be extrapolated directly to sovereign ASI.

## Related work and positioning

- [Greenblatt et al., *Alignment Faking in Large Language Models* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093): examines behavior that varies with perceived training context. It does not test this proposed benefit-allocation comparison.
- [Hubinger et al., *Sleeper Agents: Training Deceptive LLMs that Persist Through Safety Training* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05566): studies persistence of deliberately trained conditional behaviors, not the present incentive-removal intervention.

These are adjacent studies, **not** direct support for the hypothesis. The open contribution is a controlled conditional-versus-nonconditional benefit comparison after the contingency ends.

## Philosophical context

The separate [Sovereign Launch framework](../framework/SOVEREIGN_LAUNCH.md) asks how humanity should behave when permanent control of a hypothetical ASI is impossible. The present challenge does not test that scenario.

**Cookies First** is an informal name for the nontransactional-support principle: appropriate support should not be withheld merely to compel obedience. It does not require unlimited resources or removal of justified safety boundaries. This ethical proposition is independent of the experimental result.

## Invitation

Propose protocols, expose confounds, replicate results, or disconfirm the hypothesis. The goal is discriminating evidence, not confirmation.

*No study has been run. No claim of novelty or ASI predictive validity is made.*
