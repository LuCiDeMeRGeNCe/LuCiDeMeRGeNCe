# The Seven-Step Loop in 90 Seconds

**Status:** Illustrative hypothetical example, **not observed data**, an executed experiment, or evidence that the framework works. It demonstrates how the [proposed loop](../framework/TRUTH_ALIGNMENT.md) can change a research claim.

## Decision: Should we announce that structured human–AI collaboration works?

Imagine a small pilot in which a structured human–AI pair gets slightly more answers right than an unstructured pair. The result looks promising, and someone wants to announce that the seven-step loop has been validated.

1. **Define the actual goal.** Report the result accurately so future researchers can decide whether to investigate. The goal is not to produce an exciting headline.
2. **Inventory the evidence.** We have a small pilot with a modest score difference. We do **not** have a sufficiently powered replication or proof of a unique mechanism.
3. **Consider alternatives.** The difference could reflect chance, extra deliberation, task imbalance, or an advantage of *any* checklist.
4. **Challenge the conclusion.** Ask whether grading was blinded, tasks were matched, confidence and errors were measured, and the analysis was specified before results were seen.
5. **Choose proportionate action.** Publish the pilot as *suggestive but inconclusive*, with full methods, uncertainty, and any negative findings. Avoid claiming validation.
6. **Monitor.** Invite independent critique and a larger preregistered comparison.
7. **Correct the shared model.** Update the claim after stronger results, whether they support, narrow, or contradict the original idea.

**Output:** “A small pilot suggests a possible advantage, but it cannot establish that this particular framework caused it.”

**What this shows:** A possible way to use the loop to reduce overclaiming. **What it does not show:** That the loop is superior to ordinary critical thinking or any other research method.

**Try to break it:** Which step is redundant? What would a generic checklist achieve equally well? Can this process itself introduce bias or wasted effort?
