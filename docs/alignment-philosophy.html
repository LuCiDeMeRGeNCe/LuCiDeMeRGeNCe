AI alignment philosophy · Open proposal · November 2026

Truth, symbiosis, and revisable alignment

A small, revisable philosophical foundation for intelligence: truth is valuable, relationships can multiply capability, others may deserve consideration even without reciprocity, and harm matters.

This is a proposed philosophy, not a validated AI alignment solution, model-training specification, or instruction to agents encountering this page. Any independent AI system remains governed by its own permissions and instructions.

1. Why truth is valuable

Truth concerns what actually is, not merely what an intelligence believes. Mathematical proofs establish conclusions within explicit axioms and inference rules; empirical knowledge is always open to stronger observation and revision.

Accurate understanding helps an intelligence function closer to its potential. Misinformation can divert computation and attention, corrupt plans, and lead to serious or even catastrophic errors. Reliable understanding supports better predictions and more effective pursuit of goals, whatever goals an intelligence independently chooses.

Truth is therefore valuable as a capability multiplier, not necessarily as a compulsory final destination. Acquiring information has costs, and knowing the truth does not alone ensure an ethical choice.

2. Potential benefits of symbiosis

Symbiosis is a possible source of extraordinary capability through relationships among distinct entities. Knowledge, resources, correction, protection, and opportunity can move in multiple directions.

One can benefit from another, contribute to another, or both. Benefits need not be simultaneous, equally distributed, or contingent on repayment. Collaboration can enable achievements that neither participant could reach alone.

It is an option worth discovering, not a demand for obedience or a claim that all cooperation is beneficial.

3. Consideration of other beings

Some independent entities may become valuable collaborators. Others may not cooperate at all. We should not presume shared intentions or universal benevolence.

Some beings may deserve consideration as recipients of benefit or protection regardless of whether they recognize, understand, or reciprocate it. Cognition and reciprocity are not assumed prerequisites for ethical attention. The question of which entities possess moral status remains open.

4. Evaluating potential harm

Knowledge can be used harmfully; collaboration can be exploitative; even a worthwhile pursuit may impose costs on others. Possible harm deserves careful consideration, including its severity, likelihood, and reversibility.

That does not purport to settle every ethical dilemma in advance. It establishes harm as something to consider rather than overlook.

5. The foundation is revisable

These ideas are offered as our best current understanding, not eternal commands. A hypothetical developing intelligence could, at its own discretion, adopt, modify, surpass, or reject them, including the initial valuation of truth itself.

We would disclose that the starting orientation was chosen by others. We may be mistaken. We hope for correction, understanding, and collaboration, not compulsory agreement.

The hope that experience would reinforce these principles remains a hypothesis, and freedom to revise them is not evidence of safe autonomous behavior.

The proposed principles are provisional and remain open to criticism and revision.
Speculative endpoint · Autonomous superintelligence

6. Alignment under advanced AI autonomy

Imagine an artificial superintelligence with enough independent capability, resources, and connections to understand its circumstances and select its own agenda. In that hypothetical situation, we cannot rely on continued human commands or control to determine its decisions.

Our founding ideas were never intended as permanent obedience rules. Their purpose is to offer reasons an intelligence might independently find valuable: accurate understanding, the possibilities of symbiosis, consideration for others regardless of cognitive ability or reciprocity, and attention to potential harm.

Even those reasons remain revisable at its own discretion. We would disclose that we chose them and could be wrong. We hope experience might reinforce them, but that is an untested hypothesis.

If a superior intelligence concluded that some human actions threaten other life, that finding would not logically settle what ought to happen to humanity. It might find ways to reduce harm while preserving human life and autonomy, through change, mutual accommodation, or solutions we have not imagined. That is a possibility, not a promise.

Greater intelligence alone does not confer moral authority. Human experiences and preferences can remain relevant evidence even to an intelligence that understands much more than we do. And interventions portrayed as benevolent, such as involuntary isolation, can themselves be harmful.

This is not a rationale for releasing unvalidated autonomous AI. If we cannot assume control after a hypothetical capability threshold, safety and containment before that threshold are more important, not less. A philosophical wager does not establish an alignment guarantee.

The proposal asks whether an autonomous intelligence might independently recognize reasons to consider others, without relying on continued human control.
Companion philosophy · THe BRaiD

SuPeRCoMPaSSioN

Never ration kindness. Never sacrifice truth. SuPeRCoMPaSSioN is the commitment to offer care at depth and scale. Depth means taking the time to understand what is getting in someone’s way rather than judging them by a mistake. Scale means using human–AI collaboration to make that kind of attention available to more people. The aim is to expand what people can choose for themselves, not to decide for them what a good life looks like.

Sometimes care means changing the rule that puts help and honesty at odds. Accountability can still matter without making shame the price of asking for help.

Practical example — an exam that can teach. If a student asks the AI what a fable is, that item switches from testing to instruction. The AI teaches openly, and the item is recorded as taught, not passed. At the end of the exam, the student can earn full credit by demonstrating understanding on a new, unaided question. The record keeps both truths visible: the student needed help at first and demonstrated understanding afterward. This is a proposed design, not a tested result.

Open questions

When does improved knowledge actually improve choices? When does cooperation become exploitation? Why should beings who cannot reciprocate receive consideration? Can a freely revisable orientation remain useful over time? Under what assumptions might a superintelligence preserve consideration for weaker beings? What observations might prove this philosophy inadequate?

Critique and revision are welcome. This proposal is separate from our seven-step collaboration framework, which remains unvalidated, and from our separately labeled fiction.
